THE PROBLEM

Nigeria's real estate and construction markets are lucrative, but broken by design.

Every deal carries invisible risk. Buyers pay for fraud, delays, and shortages they never see coming — until it's too late.

⚠

Fraudulent Sellers

Scammers sell the same piece of land to multiple unsuspecting buyers at the same time.

⏱

Onerous Title Registration

Securing a Governor's Consent or Certificate of Occupancy (C of O) can take months, sometimes years.

₦

Hidden Transaction Costs

Buyers face unexpected fees — Deed of Assignment charges, agency commissions, surveyor costs — that only surface after they're already committed.

✊

Community Extortion (Omonile)

Local youths demand illegal cash payments before allowing construction to begin.

🏗

Demolition Risks

Buyers unknowingly purchase land marked for government infrastructure or agricultural use — and lose it to demolition.

🚧

Infrastructure Deficits

Developers promise "serviced plots" but fail to deliver basic roads, electricity, or drainage.

📈

Severe Budget Overruns

A project's actual final cost exceeds its original estimate by a critical margin.

📅

Chronic Project Delays

Continuous, systemic disruptions push a project's completion date well past its original schedule.

👷

Skilled Labour Shortage

The construction market lacks enough qualified, technically trained workers to deliver projects safely, on time, and within budget.
